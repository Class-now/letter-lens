Letter Lens

International students and residents in Japan get official letters they cannot read: pension slips, tax bills, insurance notices, immigration letters. Letter Lens explains each letter in plain English — what it is, what to do, by when, and who to ask — and, when a wrong answer could hurt, it does not guess. It sends the reader to a person.

Results

Targets were fixed before the first run: letter type ≥ 90%, deadline ≥ 90%, action coverage ≥ 85%, hand-off recall 100% with at most 1 false hand-off, explanation at Flesch–Kincaid grade ≤ 8. Held-out sets were written before the version they test was run.

How it works

  • AI does the reading. Claude identifies the kind of letter, pulls out the deadline, amount, documents and office, and writes 2–4 plain-English sentences.
  • Rules I wrote steer it. Japanese era dates are converted (令和8年 = 2026). Dates and digits that are not printed are never guessed. Document names come with the Japanese so the reader can match the paper: "residence card (在留カード)".
  • It knows when to stop. Torn or unreadable letters, scams, seizure warnings, rejections with appeal rights, and immigration letters about a pending application go to a person — with the right office named first.
Responsible AI checklist
  • No real personal data: all 44 test letters are invented.
  • Never invents a date or amount that is not printed (checked on every run).
  • High-stakes letters are handed off, not answered; false alarms are counted too, so the warning stays meaningful.
  • Scams: tells the reader not to pay and to call the ward office or the police consultation line #9110.
  • Limits: tested on one fictional ward's letters; real letters vary by city. It is a reading aid, not legal advice.
